01

Investment case

Executive summary

Eternal is two businesses fused onto one balance sheet, and the market prices them as one. A food-delivery duopoly running at a record 5.4% margin on net order value, throwing off roughly ₹2,000 crore of adjusted EBITDA a year on negative capital employed. And a quick-commerce network that in the December 2025 quarter turned adjusted EBITDA positive for the first time — ₹4 crore on ₹13,300 crore of net order value — while still growing 121% year on year. The first funds the second. That is the whole company.

We initiate at BUY with a twelve-month target price of ₹304, 25.2% above the ₹242.87 current market price. The target is a weighted blend: 30% a discounted cash flow at ₹238, 50% a sum-of-the-parts on FY30E segment metrics at ₹336, and 20% an EV/net-order-value cross-check at ₹323.

We should say the uncomfortable part first. Our discounted cash flow, at a 12.66% cost of capital and 6.5% terminal growth, produces ₹238 — marginally below the current market price. Terminal value is 76% of enterprise value, which means the DCF is a statement about FY33 wearing a valuation costume. The buy case does not rest on discounted cash flow. It rests on the sum-of-the-parts, and specifically on Blinkit being worth 1.50× its FY30E net order value. If you think that multiple is 1.20×, the blended fair value falls to roughly ₹292 and the upside halves. We would rather you attack that number than any other in this note.

The stock has fallen roughly 15% from ₹285.20 on 13 February on competitive noise — Amazon Now's expansion, Flipkart Minutes, an IPO-bound Zepto — into a quarter in which Blinkit added 211 stores, printed its first profit, took its food-delivery margin to an all-time high and accelerated food-delivery growth for a second consecutive quarter. The operating evidence and the price have moved in opposite directions. That is the setup.

Six calls

CALL 01 · HIGH CONVICTION
Blinkit's profit inflection is structural, not a print
Delhi NCR at ~3.5% and Gurgaon+Noida at ~5% of NOV are the disclosed proof that a mature Indian dark-store cluster earns real money. Consolidated Blinkit is at 0% because it is paying for 1,000 immature stores, not because the format doesn't work.
CALL 02 · HIGH CONVICTION
Throughput per store is the scoreboard, and it is holding
FY25 productivity was ₹6.71 lakh of NOV per store per day on 823 average stores. FY26E is ₹7.39 lakh on 1,800. The network doubled without diluting the cohort. That is the single most important fact in the model.
CALL 03 · HIGH CONVICTION
Food delivery is a funding source the market underrates
Margin at an all-time-high 5.4% of NOV and growth accelerating for two straight quarters against management's own "inch up towards 20%" framing. It generates the cash the expansion consumes, with no external funding required.
CALL 04 · MEDIUM CONVICTION
Model NOV, never revenue
The 1P shift grossed reported revenue up 170% year on year in Q3 FY26 with zero economic change. Consolidated EV/revenue for Eternal against Swiggy is not a comparison, it is a category error. Order value is the only denominator measured the same way on both sides.
CALL 05 · MEDIUM CONVICTION
Negative working capital is a real, underpriced asset
Grocery supplier terms are longer than grocery inventory turns. At 14 inventory days against 30 payable days, growth releases cash — worth ₹1,200–1,700 crore a year of free cash flow by the end of our forecast.
CALL 06 · CONTRARIAN
The DCF does not support this call, and we say so
₹238 against a ₹242.87 price, with 76% of value in terminal. A seven-year DCF that terminalises a business still compounding EBITDA in the mid-teens in FY33 systematically understates it. We weight it 30% and say why rather than tuning it until it agrees.
02

The business

Business overview

Eternal Limited, renamed from Zomato in March 2025, operates four reportable segments. In the December 2025 quarter they contributed ₹2,676 crore, ₹12,256 crore, ₹1,070 crore and ₹300 crore of external revenue respectively — food ordering and delivery, quick commerce, Hyperpure B2B supplies, and going-out.

That revenue split is misleading and it is worth dismantling immediately. Blinkit moved from a marketplace to an inventory-ownership model from the first quarter of FY26, and by Q3 roughly 90% of its net order value was transacted on its own inventory. Under a marketplace model the platform books commission; under 1P it books the full value of goods sold. Nothing about the economics changed. Reported consolidated revenue grew 202% year on year; management's own like-for-like adjusted revenue grew 64%. Any analysis anchored on reported revenue growth for this company in FY26 is measuring an accounting policy.

The segment that matters for value is quick commerce. It was 25.7% of FY25 revenue and, on our FY30E forecast, 100.0% — we mean by then it carries 61.1% of the sum-of-the-parts enterprise value. Food delivery carries 29.5%. Everything else is rounding, though District has genuine option value.

Leadership changed hands in the middle of this

Deepinder Goyal resigned as Managing Director and Chief Executive effective 1 February 2026. Albinder Singh Dhindsa, founder and chief executive of Blinkit, became Group CEO while stating that Blinkit remains his first priority. Read structurally rather than dramatically, that is the board formalising what the numbers already showed: the quick-commerce business is the company now, and the person who built it is running the group. It also concentrates key-person risk in one individual at precisely the moment the competitive fight intensifies. We treat it as neutral-to-positive on strategy and negative on governance concentration, and we list it under risks.

Definitional note: GOV, NOV and adjusted EBITDA

GOV is gross order value including taxes and delivery charges, before platform-funded discounts. NOV is net of them, and is the basis on which Eternal reports both Blinkit and food delivery. Swiggy reports Instamart on GOV, so the two are not comparable without adjustment. Adjusted EBITDA as Eternal defines it adds back share-based payment and deducts Ind AS 116 rent paid — so reversing to reported EBITDA means subtracting the ESOP charge and adding the rent back, which is the opposite of the instinct. The check on our model is Q3 FY26: adjusted EBITDA ₹364 crore less ₹227 crore of share-based payment plus ₹231 crore of rent gives ₹368 crore, against ₹367 crore derived independently from reported profit before tax.

03

Blinkit: the engine

Quick commerce

Blinkit is modelled bottom-up from a dark-store roll-forward: closing store count, average stores in operation, and net order value per store per day. There is no top-down growth rate anywhere in the build, because the top-down number is exactly what a reader cannot check.

The calibration is the point. FY25 net order value of ₹22,371 crore over 823 average stores implies ₹6.71 lakh per store per day. The nine months of FY26 already banked — ₹34,182 crore — plus a fourth quarter of roughly ₹14,400 crore, or 8% sequential growth on the ₹13,300 crore December print, gives ₹48,566 crore on 1,800 average stores, or ₹7.39 lakh. The network more than doubled and productivity went up, not down. Every forecast year in this model rests on that observation holding.

Why we do not model faster

Blinkit added 211 net stores in the December quarter against roughly 280 guided, held back by GRAP construction restrictions in Delhi NCR and festive operational load. Management says the shortfall opens in Q4. We take that at face value but do not extrapolate the guided stretch case: our FY27E of 3,300 stores sits 10% above the firm 3,000 guidance and 18% below the 4,000 conditional case. Margin is where we are most deliberately conservative. Reaching 6.0% of NOV only in FY33E, seven years out, when two mature clusters are already at 3.5% and 5% today, is not an aggressive assumption. It is close to the opposite.

The thing that would change our mind

Productivity per store is the load-bearing beam. If Blinkit's next 2,000 stores are structurally worse than the last 2,000 — smaller baskets, lower frequency, similar fixed cost — then ₹9.80 lakh per store per day in FY30E is wrong and so is everything downstream of it. The honest counter-evidence is that management has flagged tier-2 dilution as a live concern, and that Delhi NCR still growing net order value at roughly 55% on a broadly static store base is the only public proof that density deepens rather than exhausts. We watch NOV per store, quarterly, before anything else.

04

Food delivery: the funder

Food delivery

Food delivery is the least discussed and most valuable-per-rupee-of-capital part of this company. In the December quarter it produced ₹531 crore of adjusted EBITDA at a 5.4% margin on net order value — an all-time high — on net order value growing 16.6% year on year and gross order value growing 21.3%.

Absolute segment NOV is not separately disclosed. It is derivable: ₹531 crore at a stated 5.4% margin gives ₹9,833 crore. The same arithmetic on going-out gives ₹2,574 crore. Those two plus the disclosed ₹13,300 crore of quick-commerce NOV sum to ₹25,707 crore against the disclosed B2C total of ₹25,732 crore — a 0.1% gap explained entirely by rounding in the published margins. The reconciliation is carried as a live check on the model's Summary tab, because the entire revenue build rests on it.

Two quarters of accelerating growth off the 13.1% trough in Q1 FY26 is the fact that has not been priced. Management's framing is deliberately unexciting — growth "inches up gradually towards 20%" with no windfall catalyst — and we model 17% in FY27E and FY28E, inside that. Monetisation rises through advertising and platform fee rather than through restaurant take-rate increases, which we assume are capped by competitive and regulatory tolerance.

Why this segment carries 29.5% of the sum-of-the-parts

A duopoly with roughly 60% share, negative capital employed, low-teens-to-mid-teens net order value growth, and a margin that has risen in every recent quarter. We value it at 30× FY30E adjusted EBITDA, which is ₹145,120 crore of enterprise value, or 2.13× FY30E net order value. That is the second most attackable multiple in this note — 30× is above where global food-delivery peers have traded through a full cycle. At 25× the sum-of-the-parts falls by roughly ₹15 a share.

05

District & Hyperpure

Going-out & B2B

District is the going-out business — dining out, movies, live events — built on Zomato's restaurant graph and the ₹2,014 crore acquisition of Paytm's movie-ticketing and events assets in 2024. In Q3 FY26 net order value grew 20% year on year while the adjusted EBITDA loss widened to ₹121 crore, or −4.7% of NOV, from ₹63 crore, on upfront spend for live-event intellectual property and the launch of District Pass.

Management guides losses down sequentially to breakeven within four to six quarters, and a $3 billion net order value business at a 5% margin by FY30. Our growth path compounds ₹9,900 crore in FY26E to ₹28,123 crore in FY30E, which is that target — we have not independently forecast it so much as tested whether the stated ambition is arithmetically reachable from the current base. It is, at a 30% compound rate, which for a business growing 20% today with a new product cycle is demanding but not fanciful.

Hyperpure requires an explanation before it requires a forecast. Reported segment revenue falls in FY26 — ₹5,558 crore against ₹6,196 crore — and that looks like a shrinking business. It is not. Blinkit's move to own inventory removed sales that previously routed through third-party sellers on the platform. Restaurant-supply revenue, the actual business, grew 33% year on year in the December quarter, and the segment turned adjusted EBITDA positive at ₹1 crore. Management guides to $1 billion of topline within three years at a 4–5% margin.

06

Competitive structure

Industry position

On Datum Intelligence's 2025 data, Blinkit holds roughly 48% of Indian quick-commerce net order value, against 24% for Instamart and 22% for Zepto. The gap is not marketing. In the December quarter Blinkit turned adjusted EBITDA positive on ₹13,300 crore of net order value while Instamart lost ₹908 crore at the adjusted EBITDA line on ₹7,938 crore of gross order value, with 1,136 dark stores against Blinkit's 2,027.

The threat is not Swiggy. It is the balance sheets arriving behind it. Amazon Now, Flipkart Minutes and Reliance are all attacking the same catchments with capital that has no payback date, and Zepto raised at a $7 billion mark in late 2025 ahead of a filed IPO. None of these entrants needs quick commerce to be profitable. That is the structural risk, and it is why the stock de-rated through February rather than re-rating on a profit inflection.

Our read is that the fight is survivable but expensive, and that Eternal's advantage is funding structure rather than execution. Food delivery generates roughly ₹2,030 crore of adjusted EBITDA in FY26E and there is ₹18,911 crore of net cash after lease liabilities on the balance sheet. Swiggy raised ₹10,000 crore in a QIP at ₹375 during the same quarter it lost ₹1,065 crore. One of these companies funds expansion from operations and the other funds it from shareholders. Over a five-year subsidy war that difference compounds.

EV/revenue is a category error here

Blinkit's move to own inventory grosses up Eternal's reported revenue roughly four-fold against the equivalent marketplace figure, while Instamart remains a marketplace inside Swiggy. Comparing the two on revenue multiples produces a number that looks precise and means nothing. Order value is the only denominator measured the same way on both sides. On that basis Eternal trades at 1.55× FY27E B2C net order value against roughly 0.87× for Swiggy — a 78% premium, which is the number worth arguing about.

Working capital is the assumption to attack

Blinkit's 1P model puts inventory on the balance sheet, but it also puts supplier payables there, and grocery supply terms are longer than grocery inventory turns. At 14 inventory days against 30 payable days the business runs a cash conversion cycle of roughly −8 days, so growth releases cash rather than consuming it. By the end of our forecast that is worth ₹1,200–1,700 crore a year of free cash flow.

It is also the assumption most likely to be wrong. Management explicitly flagged a working-capital build in Q3 FY26 alongside the shift to 90% own inventory. Setting payable days equal to inventory days removes the entire benefit. Both figures are flagged levers in the model and this is the first sensitivity a sceptical reader should run.

Two things the model deliberately does not do

It does not model ESOP dilution. The share-based payment charge runs through the income statement and is credited back to reserves, but the shares issued on exercise are not added to the count, which is held flat at 965.04 crore. At roughly ₹800–1,160 crore of annual charge, that is about 0.4–0.5% of the equity a year, so the per-share values here are overstated by roughly 3–4% across the forecast. A stated, quantified omission is better than a hidden one. It also never impairs the ₹10,510 crore of goodwill, predominantly the 2022 Blinkit purchase, which permanently flatters return on invested capital.

The discounted cash flow, and why it loses the argument

Seven-year explicit forecast, FY27E to FY33E, at a 12.66% weighted average cost of capital and 6.5% terminal growth. Cost of equity is built from a peer-derived unlevered beta of roughly 0.98 relevered to 0.99, a 6.80% ten-year G-Sec and a 6.0% India equity risk premium. Enterprise value comes to ₹210,813 crore and value per share to ₹238.

That is -2.0% against the current market price. It is also 76% terminal value, which is the tell. A seven-year DCF applied to a business still compounding EBITDA in the mid-teens in FY33 terminalises it mid-S-curve and systematically understates it. That is a structural criticism of the method here, not a reason to raise the growth rate until it agrees. We weight it 30% and move on.

The reverse DCF is the more useful output. Holding this cash-flow path and this cost of capital, the market at ₹242.87 is asking Eternal to compound free cash flow in perpetuity at 6.66% — below India's roughly 10.5% nominal GDP growth. On that test the current price is not demanding. It is the terminal-value concentration, not the implied growth, that makes the DCF unreliable here.

The number this note lives or dies on

Blinkit at 1.50× FY30E net order value is 1.63× FY30E revenue. DMart — a slower-growing, lower-frequency value retailer compounding revenue in the mid-teens at an 8% EBITDA margin — trades at roughly 3.5× trailing revenue. Blinkit at that point is still growing net order value above 25%, holds roughly half of a market Datum sizes at $11.5bn and expects to more than triple by 2030, and runs at 5.1% of NOV inside a 5–6% guided band. Marking it at under half of DMart's revenue multiple is a deliberate discount for competitive risk, not an oversight. But at 1.20× the sum-of-the-parts falls to roughly ₹305 and the blended fair value to roughly ₹292, cutting the upside to about 16%. At 1.00× the rating does not survive. That is the falsification condition, stated in advance.

09

Bull vs bear

Falsifiable claims
Bull: what has to be true
  • Density deepens rather than exhausts. Delhi NCR still compounds net order value at ~55% on a broadly static store base. If that holds in the next seven metros, productivity per store rises faster than we model and the margin path pulls forward two years.
  • Competitive intensity moderates. Management's own conditional guidance is 3,500–4,000 stores by March 2027, which "would keep the pace of NOV growth north of 100%." Amazon and Flipkart are attacking many categories at once; quick commerce is not their only fight.
  • Retail media is barely started. Advertising on a grocery platform is a 3–5% of order value line at near-100% incremental margin, and is the lever that takes the category from thin retail to good retail. It is not separately modelled here.
  • Food delivery keeps compounding quietly. Two consecutive quarters of accelerating growth at a record margin, on negative capital employed, with no external funding required.
  • The balance sheet is a weapon. Net cash plus an internally funded expansion means Eternal can outlast a subsidy war that Swiggy has to raise equity to fight.
Bear: what breaks it
  • The fight simply lasts longer. This is the case that matters, and it does not require Blinkit to lose — only for three well-funded entrants to keep margins suppressed for five more years than the market assumes.
  • Tier-2 dilutes. Smaller baskets and lower frequency against store fixed costs that do not fall proportionally, because square footage tracks assortment rather than ticket size. The next 2,000 stores may be structurally worse than the last 2,000.
  • Working capital reverses. Our negative cash conversion cycle is worth ₹1,200–1,700 crore a year of free cash flow. Management flagged a working-capital build alongside the 1P shift. If payable days compress to inventory days, most of the DCF's free cash flow disappears.
  • Regulatory cost is permanent. Gig-worker social-security contributions land on a 4–6% end-state margin, not on a 20% one. That is not a footnote.
  • Multiple compression does the damage, not earnings. A platform growing 100% at 0% margin is priced on terminal margin; the same platform growing 25% at 5% is priced on earnings. That transition is rarely smooth and it is where the equity risk lives.

11

Risks

Downside

Competitive

Three entrants with balance sheets that do not require quick commerce to be profitable — Amazon Now, Flipkart Minutes, Reliance — plus a freshly funded and IPO-bound Zepto. Management's own words are the fair statement of it: "if these tactics start impacting our business, we would need to respond and that might impact our margins... quarterly profitability therefore is not guaranteed."

Operational

Store additions missed guidance in the December quarter on GRAP restrictions and festive load. A network scaling from 2,027 to 6,000 stores in four years is an execution problem of a different order, involving hiring, real estate and supply chain simultaneously. Blinkit has executed well; the base rate for this kind of scaling is not encouraging.

Regulatory

Gig-worker social-security contributions under the notified labour codes fall directly on a 4–6% terminal margin. FDI policy constrains inventory-led e-commerce and the 1P model's structure depends on continued compliance. Local restrictions — GRAP is the live example — can suspend store construction in the single densest market.

Governance and key person

The founder-CEO resigned effective 1 February 2026 and the Blinkit founder now runs the group while retaining Blinkit as his stated first priority. Strategy and value are concentrated in one person and one segment.

Model risk, stated plainly

The FY25 balance-sheet composition is an analyst allocation tying to the disclosed ₹30,317 crore of total equity. ESOP dilution of roughly 0.4–0.5% a year is not deducted, overstating per-share values by 3–4%. The peer set is two names. Goodwill is never impaired. Each of these is flagged in the workbook where it occurs.

What we watch

Monitoring

In order of how much they would move our view.

  1. Net order value per store per day, quarterly. The load-bearing assumption. FY26E is ₹7.39 lakh; we need ₹8.50 lakh by FY28E. Two consecutive quarters of decline on a growing store base would break the margin path and the sum-of-the-parts with it.
  2. Blinkit adjusted EBITDA margin sequence. Q3 FY26 was the first positive quarter at 0.0% of NOV. We need 2.0% in FY27E. A relapse into losses on competitive response is the bear case arriving.
  3. Store additions against the 3,000-by-March-2027 guidance. And whether management moves to the conditional 3,500–4,000 case, which would be the clearest signal that competitive intensity has moderated.
  4. Food delivery NOV growth and margin. Two quarters of acceleration to 16.6% and a record 5.4% margin. This segment funds everything; deterioration here matters more than its 29.5% share of the sum-of-the-parts suggests.
  5. Trade payable days. The quietest and most dangerous line. Compression toward inventory days removes most of the free cash flow in the DCF.
  6. Zepto's IPO pricing and Amazon Now's city count. The clearest external read on whether the subsidy war is escalating or normalising.
  7. Gig-worker rule notification. The point at which a 1–2% of turnover cost becomes a dated obligation rather than a risk paragraph.
